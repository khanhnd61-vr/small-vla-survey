// findings

What the papers show

  • Size barely predicts the LIBERO score. Nine models between 0.99M and 947M report 96.7 to 98.6. SimVLA (about 800M) has the highest and the 161M NanoVLA-S the lowest (78.7).
  • Models without a VLM match those with one on LIBERO. TurboVLA, SLIM, CoRP and MINERVA all reach about 97 with no VLM backbone.
  • The harder benchmarks separate them. On LIBERO-plus, SLIM holds 77.45 and Evo-Depth 69.6, while MINERVA drops to 46–56. On LIBERO-PRO, SUREFlow and SimVLA both land near 49. Only five papers report any robustness benchmark.
  • Eight of the 27 pretrain on robot data (X-VLA, FLOWER, SwiftVLA, Dita, HyperVLA, SmolVLA, Octo, and MoDE optionally). The rest train directly on benchmark demonstrations, starting from pretrained vision or language weights.
  • Headline sizes are not counted the same way. SimVLA, LLaVA-VLA and VLA-Adapter quote the VLM backbone alone. SLIM, BAKU and Octo leave out a frozen text encoder. HyperVLA and MoDE headline only the parameters active per step.
  • Training cost is rarely reported, and spans three orders of magnitude. At one end: MINERVA (about 2 h on one GPU), Patch Policy (6.5 h on one L40S) and BAKU (10.5 h on one A4000). At the other: X-VLA (64 A100s for 4 days) and DynamicVLA (32 A100s for two weeks).

// caveats

Before comparing rows

  • LIBERO protocols differ. FLOWER fine-tunes per suite, and its pretraining mix already contains LIBERO-10 and LIBERO-Goal. TurboVLA, CoRP, MINERVA and MicroVLA train one model on all four suites. Evo-1, Evo-Depth, VLA-Adapter and SUREFlow do not say.
  • Trial counts differ. Most use 50 trials per task; SmolVLA, Evo-1 and FabriVLA use 10.
  • MINERVA cannot take new instructions. It conditions on a task ID, and its own probe shows that permuting the IDs drops LIBERO from 96.75% to 6.5%.
  • MetaWorld averages are over four difficulty tiers, not over the 50 tasks. FabriVLA's 90.0 is also the best checkpoint (93k of 100k steps), chosen on the evaluation itself.
  • Octo reports only real-robot results. Any LIBERO number for Octo comes from another paper; MicroVLA's baseline table lists it at 75.1.
  • A blank LIBERO cell can hide a partial result. The column is the four-suite average. MoDE reports LIBERO-10 (0.92) and LIBERO-90 (0.91); BAKU reports LIBERO-10 (0.86) and LIBERO-90 (0.90); Patch Policy reports LIBERO-Goal only (0.96 to 0.98, goal-image conditioned); VLA-AD reports three suites (mean 94.5 with a π0.5 teacher).
  • Robot pretraining separates the top rows. X-VLA, FLOWER, SwiftVLA, Dita and HyperVLA fine-tune from a robot-pretrained checkpoint; SimVLA, SLIM, XS-VLA, TurboVLA, CoRP and MINERVA do not.
  • More small or unstated trial counts. XS-VLA uses 10 episodes per task; VLA-AD, MoDE and BAKU use 10 to 20. SimVLA, X-VLA, SwiftVLA and Dita do not state trials per task.
  • NanoVLA also has a routed variant (84.1 on LIBERO) that picks the small or large model per task. Its "296M" is an invocation-weighted average, and running it needs both models, so it is not in the table.
  • TinyVLA's headline results belong to its 1.3B variant. The two sub-billion variants have only a 5-task real-robot result (23.3% and 77.4%).
  • DynamicVLA is evaluated only on its own dynamic-object benchmark (47.06% average), so every benchmark cell is empty.
